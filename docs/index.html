Newborn Glucose Tracker

IV dextrose, formula feeds, and blood sugar over time — and the weaning of the drip.

What you're looking at

Soon after birth his blood sugar was low, so he's on an IV drip that feeds sugar straight into his blood while he learns to keep it up on his own through milk feeds. This page follows three things side by side: his blood sugar, how hard the drip is working (the GIR), and how much milk he's taking.

The plan is to slowly turn the drip down — to wean it — as his feeds take over, without letting his sugar dip too low. ✓ Good progress looks like: steady blood sugar while the drip steps down and the feeds climb.

Now that the drip is off: a companion tool works out how much aluminum the IV put in, how his body clears it, and how that compares to the toxic threshold and to vaccines — with a full research memo.

Words explained

Blood sugar (glucose) — how much sugar is in his blood, measured in mg/dL. Too low is the problem being managed.

GIR (glucose infusion rate) — how much sugar the IV drip delivers for his body size, in mg/kg/min. Think of it as how hard the drip is working. The goal is to bring it down to zero (off the drip).

Dextrose / “D20” — the sugar dissolved in the IV fluid. “D20” means 20% dextrose. IV rate is how fast that fluid runs, in mL/hour.

Fortification (cal/oz) — how calorie-rich his milk is made; 24 cal/oz is richer than 20. Breast milk is added alongside the formula.

Weaning — slowly turning the drip down as his own feeds keep his sugar up. “High support” — a GIR above about 8, meaning he's leaning on the drip a lot.

How the numbers are calculated & where they come from

GIR (Glucose Infusion Rate) = (%dextrose × IV rate mL/hr) ÷ (6 × weight kg) — the Brigham & Women's / CWN neonatal glucose pathway, referencing Chowning & Adamkin, J Perinatol 2015. This baby's weight is .

Weaning rule (first 48h, same pathway): consider weaning GIR by 0.5 if BG > 50, by 1.0 if BG > 60. A GIR above ~8 mg/kg/min is considered high support.

IV dextrose grams/hr = IV rate × (%dextrose ÷ 100). Feed glucose = volume × carbohydrate per 100 mL. Formula carbs scale with each feed's fortification (Enfamil Gentlease base 10.8 g carb per 100 Cal; a 24 cal/oz feed carries more carb per mL than a 20 cal/oz feed); breast milk adds ~7 g carb per 100 mL. These are estimates — dietary lactose is digested to glucose + galactose, so feeds aren't a 1:1 IV-dextrose equivalent.

Fluid cap ≈ 100 mL/kg/day (guideline ceiling on combined IV + oral intake).

Sources provided by the family: Brigham/CWN "WNH G.1" neonatal glucose pathway; Giouleka et al., Children 2023; Rozance & Hay, Matern Health Neonatol Perinatol 2016. This is a personal tracking aid, not medical advice — the NICU team's readings and orders are authoritative.
