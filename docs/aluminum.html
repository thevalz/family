Is the FDA's 4–5 mcg/kg/day limit an oral or an IV number?

It is intravenous (parenteral) — not oral.
The FDA dextrose label (§5.6) applies the limit to patients who “receive parenteral levels of aluminum at greater than 4 to 5 mcg/kg/day.” Route is everything, because how much aluminum actually reaches the blood depends on it:
Oral / feeds ≈ 0.1–0.3% absorbed Intravenous ≈ 100% (bypasses the gut) Intramuscular (vaccines) ≈ high, but released slowly over weeks

Why you can't read the vaccine mcg/kg against the 4–5 line directly. The FDA's 4–5 mcg/kg/day limit was set for intravenous products — aluminum delivered straight into the bloodstream, every day, in babies whose kidneys can't keep up. Vaccine aluminum is intramuscular: it sits in the muscle and dissolves into the blood slowly over weeks while the kidneys clear it daily. So the injected mcg/kg shown above is not the daily amount in his blood — the peak systemic level is a small fraction of it. FDA's own pharmacokinetic model (Mitkus 2011) found the body burden of aluminum across the whole schedule stays well below the safety limit. A per-visit mcg/kg-vs-IV-limit comparison overstates the exposure; it's shown here only so you can see the injected amounts, not as a “toxic” verdict.

What this tool is — and isn't. A personal calculator, not medical advice. Your NICU team and pediatrician have his real records and are the authority.

Key assumptions, stated plainly:

• The 25 mcg/L is the label's maximum, not a measurement of his lots — true exposure is likely lower.
• This counts the dextrose only; any TPN additives (usually the bigger aluminum source) are excluded unless you enter them above.
• The clearance / body-burden curve is a literature-based estimate, not a lab value. Only a serum/urine aluminum test measures him directly.
• Vaccine aluminum is intramuscular slow-release, a different route from the IV drip — see the note above.
• Vaccine per-dose amounts are typical package-insert values and vary by brand.
